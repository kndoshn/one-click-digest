City council approves downtown bike lanes

The city council voted seven to two on Tuesday to build protected bike lanes on four downtown streets, ending a debate that lasted more than a year.

Supporters pointed to five years of crash data showing that most serious injuries to cyclists happened on the same four streets, and to a survey in which a majority of residents asked for safer routes to work.

Business owners on Market Street had asked the council to keep parking in front of their shops. The final plan moves about forty spaces to side streets and adds two loading zones for deliveries.

Construction is scheduled to begin next spring and to finish within eighteen months. The project will cost about twelve million dollars, most of it covered by a state transportation grant.

The two council members who voted against the plan said they supported bike lanes but wanted more time to study the effect on bus routes. The transit agency said it would publish an analysis before construction.

The mayor, who proposed the plan, said the city would report crash numbers on the new lanes every six months so that residents could judge whether the change made the streets safer.
