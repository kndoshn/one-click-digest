The Harlow Bay city council approved a $412 million budget for the 2027 fiscal year on Monday night, voting six to three after a four-hour meeting that drew more than two hundred residents.

The budget restores weekend hours at all five public libraries, which were cut to weekdays only in 2025 to save about $1.8 million a year. Library director Amara Okafor said Saturday visits had made up nearly a third of all visits before the cut. The branches will reopen on Saturdays in February and on Sundays in April, once the library hires twelve part-time staff.

To pay for the libraries and a 4 percent raise for city employees, the council slowed the road repair program. The city had planned to repave 38 miles of streets next year; the budget funds 27. Public works director Tom Reyes warned that deferring repairs usually raises costs later, because cracked roads deteriorate faster once water gets under the surface. He estimated that the delay could add $3 million to future repair bills.

Property taxes will not rise. Instead, the budget draws $6.5 million from the city's reserve fund, leaving the reserve at 11 percent of annual spending. The city's own policy calls for a reserve of at least 10 percent, and the finance office said a second year of withdrawals would push it below that line.

Council member Diane Feld, who voted against the budget, said the city was "spending its savings to avoid a hard conversation about revenue." Mayor Luis Ortega defended the plan, saying the reserve exists for years when the economy is slow and that sales tax revenue had come in 2 percent below forecast.

The budget also sets aside $750,000 for a pilot program that will send mental health counselors, instead of police officers, to some emergency calls. The program will start in two neighborhoods in the spring and will be evaluated after one year.

The new fiscal year begins on January 1.
