The fictional Larkspur Commuter 2 costs $1,899, about $300 less than most electric bikes with a similar motor and battery. After three weeks of riding it to work and on weekend errands, we think it is a good choice for most commuters, with one exception.

The motor is quiet and helps smoothly up to 20 miles per hour, the legal limit for this class of bike in most U.S. states. On the steepest hill on our route, a 9 percent grade, it kept us at about 12 miles per hour without much effort. The hydraulic disc brakes stopped the bike confidently in the rain.

Larkspur says the 500-watt-hour battery lasts "up to 60 miles." In our testing, with the assist mostly on the middle setting and a 180-pound rider, we got 38 miles from a full charge. Riders who use the lowest setting on flat roads will probably get more, but we would not plan on 60. A full charge took about five hours with the included charger.

The flaw is the display. In direct sunlight it is hard to read, and the buttons are small enough that we often pressed the wrong one while wearing gloves. Larkspur told us a software update in the spring will add larger numbers, but it did not say whether the hardware will change.

The bike weighs 54 pounds, which is typical for its class but heavy to carry up stairs. Fenders, lights and a rear rack come standard; on many competing bikes they cost extra.

Larkspur offers a two-year warranty on the frame and electronics, shorter than the five years some rivals offer on the frame. The company sells only online, and it delivers the bike mostly assembled. Our bike arrived with a loose front brake caliper, which took about 10 minutes to fix with the included tools.

Bottom line: if you ride in daylight and can live with the display, the Commuter 2 is one of the better values we have tested this year. If you need to carry your bike or want a longer warranty, look elsewhere.
